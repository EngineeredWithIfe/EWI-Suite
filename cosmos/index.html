EWI Cosmos — field guide

A real gravitational sandbox. Every body pulls on every other by Newton's law of universal gravitation, integrated with a symplectic leapfrog so orbits stay stable over thousands of years. Units are astronomer-friendly: astronomical units (AU), years, and solar masses (M☉), so a body at 1 AU around a 1 M☉ star completes one orbit per year.

Getting started

  • Load a Scenario on the left — the Solar System, a binary star, Saturn's rings, a galaxy disk, or a head-on collision.
  • Pick a material and mass, switch to the Launch tool, then click-drag on empty space to fling a new world. The arrow you drag is its initial velocity.
  • Click any body with the Select tool to open the inspector — rename it, change its mass or material, boost or reverse its velocity, pin it in place, or delete it.

What is simulated

  • Gravity — full N-body attraction with softening near contact.
  • Collisions & material — bodies that touch merge inelastically, conserving momentum and mass; the impact energy heats the survivor.
  • Climate — stars radiate a luminosity set by their mass (L ∝ M³·⁵). Each world's temperature follows the stellar flux it receives, and the green ring marks where liquid water could exist.
